🧊 Cooling Method Comparison — Superconducting LINAC Cryogenics

Compares four common helium cooling strategies used in superconducting RF (SRF) LINAC systems. Key metrics include operating temperature, saturation pressure, Carnot-limit COP, practical refrigerator efficiency, and achievable SRF cavity Q₀ for bulk-niobium cavities at 20 MV/m. Practical COP assumes ≈ 30 % of ideal Carnot, representative of large-scale helium refrigerators.

Refrigerator Efficiency — Carnot vs Practical (W_in / W_removed)

Lower is better. Bars show the Carnot lower bound; outlined bars show the practical achievable value at scale. High Carnot penalty drives preference for higher operating temperatures where possible.

Operating Pressure at Nominal Set-point

Logarithmic scale. Sub-atmospheric pressures (2 K, 1.8 K) require vacuum integrity on large cryomodule systems, adding engineering complexity compared with over-pressure 4.2 K operation.

🔬 He-4 Saturation Properties — NIST Reference Data

Source: NIST Chemistry WebBook, SRD 69 — Helium-4 saturation properties. Temperature range: 2.177 K (λ-point, He-I / He-II boundary) to 5.195 K (critical point, T_c). Charts are drawn from the 12-point table in the Data Reference tab. For precision calculations, use NIST WebBook or NIST REFPROP.

Saturation Pressure P vs Temperature

Vapour pressure rises steeply from 5 kPa at the λ-point to 227 kPa at the critical point. Used to set operating pressure for any desired temperature.

Liquid and Vapour Density vs Temperature

Liquid density decreases while vapour density increases; the two converge at the critical point (ρ_c = 69.6 kg/m³). Density ratio determines vapour fraction in two-phase systems.

Enthalpy of Vaporisation h_fg vs Temperature

Latent heat decreases from ≈ 21 kJ/kg at the λ-point to zero at the critical point. Critical to sizing He recovery compressors and phase separators.

Liquid Specific Heat c_p,L vs Temperature

c_p rises sharply near the λ-point and diverges at the He-II transition. The anomalously high c_p of He-II provides excellent thermal buffering in SRF cryomodules.

🔥 Cryomodule Heat Budget — Typical 8-Cavity 1.3 GHz SRF Cryomodule

Indicative heat-load breakdown based on published TESLA/ILC-style cryomodule performance data. Values are representative of steady-state operation at 20 MV/m. Absolute figures scale with cavity count and gradient; relative fractions are broadly applicable across modern 1.3 GHz designs.

Heat Load at 2 K Level (W per cryomodule)

Dynamic RF load dominates at high gradient. Static contributions (support conduction, radiation, HOM absorbers, coupler) set the floor even with RF off.

Heat Load at 70 K Intercept Level (W per cryomodule)

The 70 K thermal shield intercepts the bulk of room-temperature radiation and conduction, reducing 2 K loads dramatically. Aluminium shields with LN₂ or He-gas cooling are typical.

AC Wall-Plug Power per Temperature Level (per cryomodule)

Same physical heat load translates to very different electricity consumption across temperature levels because of the Carnot penalty. This chart makes the 2 K penalty visible — it dominates total refrigerator power despite being the smallest heat load.
